First Session

Connected DR. GREGORY BRAID, LPC
Psychological horror Written by Scott Downes Script complete

Every one of Dr. Gregory Braid's online patients has the same recurring nightmare: a purple-lit motel off I-75, a long hallway, footsteps behind them. Tonight, Josie is checking in.

The Lark Motor Inn VACANCY Exit 306 · I-75 · North Georgia

The place

A cheap motel off the interstate, somewhere between Calhoun and nothing in particular. Kudzu has eaten the embankment behind the parking lot. Across the highway, a barn roof still says SEE ROCK CITY. The purple lights come on before the sun is all the way down.

Checkout's eleven. Leave the key on the bed. Nobody comes to the desk. Cars left past noon get towed. They tow Thursdays.

Everything else happens in little windows on a laptop screen, in a beige room with a fiddle-leaf fig that might be real.

The people

Four first sessions

  • The therapist

    Dr. Gregory Braid

    Fifties. Pressed shirt, clean glasses, the face of somebody who has never once interrupted you. He moved his whole practice online during COVID and has come to love it. After every session he makes a cup of Lapsang souchong and sits with his notes.

  • The aesthetician

    Laura

    Twenty-four, and really good with eyebrows. She just signed a lease on her own place in a Cartersville strip mall and already has the name picked out. She's never done therapy before. I kind of thought you'd tell me.

  • The tow driver

    Dana

    Late thirties. Drives a wrecker for Etowah Wrecker Service, has a seventh grader who plays the trombone, and is in therapy because a judge said so. Huge fan of consequences.

  • The runner

    Josie

    Twenty-eight. Asian-American, East Tennessee born and raised, nine hours into the drive home to Knoxville with everything she owns in the back of a Corolla. She told her mom not to make a fuss. Her mom has already bought the crepe paper.

Was it a vision, or a waking dream? Fled is that music. Do I wake or sleep?

John Keats, "Ode to a Nightingale"

The song

Something worth singing about

Every Nightingale story finds its light inside the dark, and First Session is the darkest place the series goes. It's a story about people who can't get out of one night, and a man who has made very sure they can't.

It is also, in the end, a story about letting go, and about who gets to decide when a session is over.
